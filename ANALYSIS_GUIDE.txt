NVIDIA PROCESS METRICS - ANALYSIS GUIDE
========================================

1. INPUT FILES

gpu_telemetry.csv
  Device-level NVML telemetry. power_mw is board power, not process or kernel
  power. timestamp_ns is the timestamp used to align it with CUPTI Activity.

kernel_activity.csv
  CUPTI Activity intervals from the profiled process. Each record identifies a
  kernel or memory-copy operation and provides start_ns, end_ns, context,
  stream, and correlation information.

pm_sampling.csv
  Device-global CUPTI PM Sampling intervals and hardware metrics. Its samples
  can overlap a kernel, partly cover it, or have no overlap with it.

2. ENERGY ANALYSIS

analyze_kernel_energy.py produces:

kernel_energy.csv
  One attributed-energy estimate per kernel event.

energy_windows.csv
  Energy, device power, active kernel time, and utilization for each analysis
  window. The configured --window-ms must match the window used for comparison.

energy_summary.csv
  Total device energy, attributed energy, unassigned energy, and correlations.

energy_timeline.png
  Compares attributed energy and kernel-active utilization by time window.

utilization_energy_correlation.png
  Scatter plot of active utilization versus attributed energy or power.

Interpretation:
  - "Assigned" energy is algorithmically attributed to kernels that overlap a
    telemetry interval.
  - "Unassigned" energy belongs to intervals without active kernels, capture
    boundaries, or periods excluded by the data.
  - With --idle-power-mw 0, call the result "Attributed Device Energy". It is
    not direct physical energy measured per kernel.
  - A nearly flat utilization plot is normal for a saturated benchmark. It does
    not prove that every kernel has the same power or energy.

3. PM SAMPLING ANALYSIS

kernel_pm_metrics.csv
  PM coverage and weighted PM metrics per kernel.

pm_coverage_pct = 100
  Valid PM samples cover the full kernel duration.

pm_coverage_pct = 0
  No valid PM sample overlaps that kernel. Do not use PM metric columns for
  that kernel.

0 < pm_coverage_pct < 100
  The kernel has partial PM coverage. Its metrics are estimates over only the
  covered portion.

pm_coverage_timeline.png
  Each blue point is one kernel. The X axis is the kernel start time relative
  to the first kernel. The Y axis is PM coverage. Points at 0% identify kernels
  inside a PM sampling gap; points at 100% have complete PM coverage.

pm_sampling_gaps.png
  Each red point is a gap between valid PM samples. The X axis locates the gap
  relative to the first kernel. Negative X values occur before the first kernel
  because PM Sampling starts before the workload. The Y axis is logarithmic:
  10^-1 ms is 0.1 ms, 10^0 ms is 1 ms, 10^1 ms is 10 ms, and 10^2 ms is
  100 ms. Only gaps at or after 0 seconds can affect kernel PM coverage.

pm_kernel_summary.csv
  Use "kernels_with_pm_coverage" and
  "kernels_with_at_least_99_9_pct_coverage" to compare runs. Higher values are
  better. Compare PM gap count, total gap duration, and largest gap from the
  pm_sampling.log continuity section.

4. PM-WEIGHTED ENERGY ATTRIBUTION

analyze_kernel_energy.py accepts --pm-kernel-metrics with the
kernel_pm_metrics.csv generated by analyze_kernel_pm_metrics.py. Its default
weight is sm__inst_executed_realtime.avg.pct_of_peak_sustained_elapsed.

For each NVML energy subinterval, PM weighting is used only if every concurrent
kernel has a valid, positive weight and at least 99.9% PM coverage. Otherwise,
the subinterval falls back to equal shares among active kernels. This avoids
giving all energy to a covered kernel when a concurrent kernel has no PM data.

kernel_energy.csv adds:
  pm_coverage_pct                    PM coverage of the kernel.
  pm_energy_weight                   PM metric used as its weight.
  pm_weighted_energy_mj              Energy assigned by PM weighting.
  equal_share_fallback_energy_mj     Energy assigned by time fallback.

The PM-weighted model is an attribution heuristic. It still starts from NVML
device energy and cannot remove PM activity from another concurrent process.

5. RECOMMENDED REPORTING LANGUAGE

Use "Attributed Device Energy per Kernel" for energy calculated from NVML.
Use "PM coverage" for the portion of a kernel duration covered by valid PM
samples. Do not describe either value as direct energy measured inside an SM
or a kernel.
